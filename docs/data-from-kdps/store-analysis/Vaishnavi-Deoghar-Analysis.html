The headline

A healthy, busy store — sitting on far too much stock

The store sells well: ₹1.67 crore in the year, over 5,300 bills, with a loyal base of repeat customers. The problem is not selling — it's buying and stock. Roughly 1.8 years of inventory is on the shelves, most of it not moving, and discounting is quietly eating the margin. Fixing the stock and discount discipline is where the money is.

What's good here

  • Consistent footfall — 350–590 bills every month, no dead months.
  • Bill value rising — average bill up ~11% this year; customers are buying more per visit.
  • Strong festive uplift — September alone did ₹19.7 L. The calendar is predictable and can be planned for.

What to watch

  • October dipped to ₹8 L — worth checking why (stock gap? offer timing?) so it isn't repeated.
  • Units per bill ~2.4 — room to grow add-ons (socks, innerwear, belts) at the counter.
  • Growth is flat year-on-year — the lever for more profit is cost and stock, not just top-line.
2 · The big one — inventory

₹1.80 crore is locked in stock, and most of it isn't moving

This is the single biggest opportunity in the business. The store is carrying about 1.8 years of inventory. In fashion, where styles age fast, healthy stores turn their stock 3–4 times a year (≈3 months on hand). Carrying this much means cash is trapped and goods are quietly going out of season.

78% of stock value (₹1.41 Cr) has not sold a single piece in the last 14 months. Not one size, not one colour. Some of this is new 2026 stock that deserves time — but a large chunk is genuinely old and stuck.

3 · Discounting & margin

One-third of ticket value is given away — and it's uneven

The store buys at roughly 59% of MRP (about a 41% mark-up). But after discounts, customers pay only 67% of the ticket value on average. On items that were actually sold, the average discount is 24%. That is a lot of margin leaving the building, and it's not applied consistently.

The discount isn't controlled

  • Realisation swings by month — from 86% (April) down to 60% in the August clearance. The clearance calendar is heavy.
  • It swings by staff — best salesperson collects 78% of ticket, the lowest 68%. On ~₹25 L of sales each, that gap is ~₹2.4 L a year.
  • It swings by brand — Jockey holds 90% of price; Van Heusen and Spykar collect only ~66–69%.
  • Gifts add up — ₹28 L of merchandise (backpacks, belts, trolleys) given away as gifts, plus ~8,200 carry bags.
Why this matters: with a 41% mark-up, a 24% discount on a sold item leaves only a thin margin — and the deepest-discounted brands may be near break-even after gifts and returns. Tightening discounting by even 5 percentage points on the ₹2.3 Cr of goods sold is worth ₹10 lakh+ a year, straight to profit.

6 · Data & system health

The numbers are hard to trust — because the data is messy

While analysing, several data problems showed up that make it difficult for anyone to get a clean answer from the current system. These are fixable, and fixing them is the foundation for everything above.

  • The same brand is recorded under many names. Louis Philippe appears as LP, LY, LR and LOUIS PHILIPPE; Van Heusen as VAN HEUSEN, VH, VS, VD, VF, VX. So no brand total can be trusted until it's manually merged — which is how this report had to be built.
  • The sales report and the stock report don't use the same brand names. Sales says "LP", stock says "LOUIS PHILIPPE". This breaks the link between what sold and what's in stock — the most important link for buying decisions.
  • The same item is dated differently in two places. The stock workbook holds the same items across two sheets, and 34% of items (3,007 of 8,805) carry a different season label in each — e.g. SS24 vs SS-24, blank vs AW-21, and often a date (2026-03-24) where a season should be. So the same shirt has two different "ages" depending on which sheet you read — and ageing can't be tracked reliably.
  • Totals and dead rows are mixed into the live data, and 24 items show cost higher than MRP (e.g. Van Heusen jeans costed at ₹4,375 against a ₹2,499 tag). None of it is fatal, but it shows the data isn't being checked. (Full error list shared separately.)
  • Shopping carry-bags are recorded as a "brand" / sold item (~8,200 a year), inflating unit counts unless cleaned out.
Why this is the real prize: every recommendation in this report — dead stock, buying, discounts, brand decisions — depends on clean, connected data. Today the owner cannot see stock-turn, dead-stock %, realisation, or true brand performance without someone rebuilding it by hand. A system that keeps one clean brand list, links sales to stock, and shows these numbers live turns this once-a-year manual exercise into an everyday dashboard.
7 · What to do — in priority order

Seven moves, biggest cash impact first

1 Free the cash trapped in dead stock

Run a structured exit for the ₹38 L of truly dead stock and the ₹43 L aging 2025 stock — deep clearance, bundling, moving goods to other KDPS stores where they may sell, and returning sale-or-return goods to vendors before deadlines lapse. Start with the worst: the 377 Van Heusen trolleys, big-size suits, and waist 42–44 formals.

↳ Potential ₹25–40 L of working capital freed within two clearance cycles.

2 Buy tighter — fix the real problem

61% of stock value is in styles that never sold. Cut the open-to-buy on slow, overstocked brands (Spykar, Status Quo, and the deep end of Van Heusen/LP), shorten size curves (stop over-buying XXL / waist 42–44 / large-size suits), and shift that budget to fast turners — Jockey and innerwear, U.S. Polo, and the small high-velocity brands.

↳ Moving from ~0.5 to even 1.0 stock-turn could run the same sales on ₹50–70 L less inventory.

3 Put discounting under control

Set floor prices / max-discount per brand, measure each salesperson on realisation (price held), not just sales value, and coach the lower performers toward the best. Review whether the ₹28 L of gifts (trolleys, backpacks, belts) actually drive enough sales to justify them.

↳ Tightening discount by 5 points ≈ ₹10 L+/year; closing the staff gap ≈ ₹2–3 L/year.

4 Clean up the brand & product master

One canonical name per brand, used identically in billing and stock. Merge LP/LY/LR, the Van Heusen codes, etc. This is the foundation that makes every report trustworthy and links sales to stock.

↳ Enables accurate buying, ageing and brand decisions — the basis of moves 1, 2 & 6.

5 Switch on the customer base

Use the phone numbers you already capture: monthly WhatsApp to lapsed customers, festive reminders, and a simple VIP perk for the top 5% who drive a fifth of sales.

↳ Near-zero cost; repeat customers already drive ~93% of sales.

6 Rebalance the assortment

Stock is ~74% menswear. Womenswear (kurti sets) and innerwear are turning comparably or better — test growing them. Treat innerwear (Jockey) as a deliberate footfall + full-margin engine, placed and stocked to repeat.

↳ Higher turns and full-price mix without adding total stock.

7 Manage by a monthly dashboard

Track five numbers every month: stock-turn, dead-stock %, realisation %, sell-through by brand, and repeat-customer rate. None of these are visible today — yet they are exactly what drives the profit levers above.

↳ Turns this one-off analysis into an everyday operating habit.

How this was prepared. Built from three files the client provided: the full-year sale report (1 Apr 2025–31 Mar 2026), the current-period sale report (1 Apr–13 Jun 2026), and the stock-on-hand as on 12 Jun 2026. Figures are computed directly from those files. Bill-level fields were filled down within each bill; carry-bags and free gifts were separated from sold goods; brand families were merged using barcode matches between sales and stock.

Honest caveats. "Sold in 14 months" is matched at exact size/colour barcode, so newly-arrived 2026 stock will correctly look "unsold" — which is why dead stock is split by age. The ~41% mark-up and "1.8 years of stock" use the cost and MRP in the stock file and a standard cost-of-goods estimate; treat margin figures as directional, not audited accounts. Brand-family groupings are inferred from the data and should be confirmed with the store. Rupee impacts in the action plan are estimates to size the opportunity, not guarantees.
