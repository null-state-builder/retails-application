Issue 2

The same item carries a different season in the two sheets

The workbook has two sheets listing the same 8,805 in-stock items. For 3,007 of them (34%), the "Season" — which tells you how old the stock is — does not match between the sheets. Here's what's behind those 3,007:

The takeaway: roughly a third are harmless formatting differences, but over 1,000 items have a date instead of a season, and another 640 are blank on one side. Together that means the store cannot automatically sort stock by age — and ageing is what drives markdowns and clearing dead stock. The full item-by-item list (with both season values) is in the attached Excel.
Issue 3

Totals and dead rows are mixed into the live data

  • Two sheets for the same stock. One sheet lists the full catalogue (33,383 rows — including 24,576 items with zero stock), the other lists only the 8,805 items actually in stock. Keeping both, with different season labels, is how the mismatch above creeps in.
  • A grand-total row is buried inside the data — a row with no barcode carrying the whole-store total (13,375 pieces, ₹1.80 Cr). If anyone sums the quantity column without spotting it, the total is double-counted.
  • Zero-stock items sit in the same list as live stock — fine for a catalogue, but it inflates counts and slows down anyone reading the file.
What to do: none of this needs a big project. The lasting fix is for the billing/stock system to keep one record per item with a single, validated season and a cost that can never exceed MRP — so these errors can't be entered in the first place. Until then, the 24 cost errors can be corrected today, and the season field standardised to one format.
