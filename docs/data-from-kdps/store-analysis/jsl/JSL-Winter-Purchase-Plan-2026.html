The headline

The store already has too much stock — so buy less, but buy the right winter things

The shop is full. It is holding more than a year's worth of stock, and a big part of it is last winter's clothes that never sold. So winter is not the time for a big new order. It is the time to clear the old stock, and spend a small, careful budget on the few things that really sell in winter — mostly festival and Indian wear, plus a small winter-clothing range.

This is the most important thing in the report. Look at the discount column. Men's jeans, shirts and t-shirts are sold at 28–42% off — the shop has too many, so they go on sale and make almost no profit. But sarees, kurti sets and salwar suits sell at just 3–4% off — almost full price — and they sell faster. Indian wear is where the profit is. Spend the winter money there.
Buy common sizes only. Most sales are in the middle sizes: waist 30 to 34 for jeans and trousers, and M, L, XL for shirts and t-shirts. Very small and very large sizes get stuck and end up as old stock — buy very few of those.
2 · The problem — too much old stock

The shop is holding more than a year of stock

This is why we must be careful with new buying. The shop is carrying about 15 months of stock. A healthy clothes shop, where fashion changes fast, keeps only 3 to 4 months. Too much stock means cash is stuck on the shelves and clothes slowly go out of season.

₹1.44 crore of the stock is already 6 months old or more. And ₹1.26 crore of that is last winter's clothes — still sitting unsold, about to go into a second winter. This is the money to free up first.

Why so little winter clothing? The shop is still holding about 1,650 winter pieces from last year (sweatshirts, jackets, winter tops), and most are unsold. That tells us this is not a heavy-winter town — so don't over-buy heavy winter wear. Re-display the plain ones that don't look dated (thermals, simple sweaters, mufflers, shawls), and buy only a small fresh, fashionable lot on top.

When to buy — finish by October

Stock must be on the floor before each rush. The big dates this year are around Dussehra (20 Oct), Diwali (8 Nov) and Chhath (15 Nov), then the wedding season and the cold months (Dec–Jan). So the buying runs July to October.

5 · What to do now

Five simple steps

1 Clear the old stock first

Start a clearance now, before the festivals, on the ₹1.44 crore of old stock — last winter's clothes first. Use the festival and sale crowds to turn it back into cash.

↳ Frees ₹1.1–1.3 crore of cash and empties the shelves for new stock.

2 Spend that money on festival & Indian wear

Put more than half the winter budget into fresh sarees, kurti sets, salwar suits, lehengas and wedding wear. This is what sells at full price in this shop.

↳ Best profit, and the festive + wedding season is the right time.

3 Buy only a little men's western

The shop already has too many jeans, shirts and t-shirts. Buy only fresh winter styles (full-sleeve shirts, dark jeans, light jackets) in common sizes (waist 30–34, M/L/XL). Let the rest sell down.

↳ Stops the over-stock problem from getting bigger.

4 Keep the winter-clothing order small

Last winter's sweaters and jackets are still here. Re-display the plain ones, and buy only a small, fashionable fresh lot. Don't over-buy heavy winter wear.

↳ Avoids repeating last year's left-over winter stock.

5 Don't let total stock grow

For every new piece that comes in, one old piece should go out (sold or cleared). The aim is to end winter with less stock than today — about 11 months' worth, not 15.

↳ More cash, fewer markdowns, a fresher shop.
